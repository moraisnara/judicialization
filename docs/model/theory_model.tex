\title{What a Filing Reveals:\\ A Companion Model for the Electoral Costs of
Judicialization}
\author{}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Adversarial electoral litigation widens the top-two margin in Brazilian
mayoral races. This note asks what mechanism can produce that, and finds that
the natural candidate cannot. In the transfer class of campaign-attack models,
an attack moves support from its target to its sender; under the attack
direction those models derive and document, the map is a contraction on the
top-two margin, so it cannot generate the result. The alternative developed
here turns on what a filing reveals. A filing is a noisy signal about a
candidate's type and a precise signal about a candidate's rank, because the
filer is optimizing and targets the leading opponent. Litigation therefore
informs voters about position more than about quality. One parameter movement
then delivers three comparative statics. The posterior gap between the top two
becomes less dispersed, so rank reversals become rarer and the expected margin
widens. The posterior variance of every candidate's valence rises, so an
ambiguity-averse voter is readier to leave the valid ballot, which under
compulsory voting means a blank or null vote. And because an unsettled record
disperses voters' impressions, the runner-up's supporters are selected on
idiosyncratic distaste for the leader only where the leader is an incumbent --
which is what separates open from contested seats.
\end{abstract}

\section{Environment}
\label{sec:env}

One mayoral race. Candidates $j = 1,\dots,J$ are indexed by pre-shock support
$s^\circ_1 \ge s^\circ_2 \ge \dots \ge s^\circ_J$, and there is a continuum of
voters of mass one. Voter $i$'s payoff from candidate $j$ is
\[
  u_{ij} \;=\; \hat\theta_j \;-\; \tfrac{\rho}{2}\,\hat V_i(\theta_j)
          \;+\; \varepsilon_{ij},
\]
where $\theta_j$ is a common-value valence nobody observes and
$\varepsilon_{ij} \sim N(0, v_\varepsilon)$, drawn independently across voters
and candidates, is a durable idiosyncratic taste the voter knows.
$\hat V_i(\theta_j)$ is the posterior variance of $\theta_j$; every voter holds
the same one, so we write $\hat V(\theta_j)$. The coefficient $\rho \ge 0$
prices it: the term is the mean--variance reduction of a weight-of-evidence
preference, under which thin evidence about a candidate is itself a reason to
withhold a choice \citep{ghirardato2006}. At $\rho = 0$ the voter is an
expected-utility maximizer and every statement below that runs through $\rho$
vanishes, which is what makes the ambiguity content testable rather than
assumed.

Beliefs about valence start at $\theta_j \sim N(m_j, 1/h_j)$: $m_j$ is the
candidate's reputation and $h_j$ measures how settled the record is. An
incumbent who has governed for four years has a high $h$; a first-time
candidate has a low one.

How settled a record is also governs how much voters agree about it.

\begin{assumption}[Impressions]
\label{as:impressions}
Before the campaign, voter $i$'s impression of candidate $j$ is
$m_j + \zeta_{ij}$ with $\zeta_{ij} \sim N(0, \kappa_\zeta/h_j)$, independent
across voters and candidates and independent of $\varepsilon$. Impressions
decide which candidate a voter starts out supporting. They do not survive the
campaign: a filing wave is public and draws local attention, so by the ballot
stage the common posterior $\hat\theta_j$ has displaced them, and the voter's
ballot-stage payoff carries $\varepsilon_{ij}$ but not $\zeta_{ij}$.
\end{assumption}

A four-year record leaves little room for disagreement about what a mayor is
like; a fresh candidate leaves a great deal. That is the content of
$\kappa_\zeta/h_j$. The second half of the assumption is the half that does
work: it separates what sorts voters into camps before the campaign
(impression plus taste) from what they act on at the ballot (taste alone).
It is not free, and Section~\ref{sec:scope} says what it costs.

Let $\bar b$ denote the payoff from a blank or null vote, which is what
refusing to endorse anyone is worth once the ballot carries no instrumental
value.

Treatment is $\lambda$, the intensity of adversarial filings in the race, and
it is the object the shift-share instrument shifts. Two institutional facts
fix the timing and the margin of adjustment. Candidate registration closes on
15 August while adversarial filings run through October, so the candidate set
is predetermined with respect to $\lambda$. And voting is compulsory
(CF/88 art.~14 \S1), so a voter who wants out of the contest adjusts the mark
rather than the attendance: the exit option is a blank or null vote.

Throughout, $\varphi$ and $\Phi$ are the standard normal density and
distribution function, and $\bar\Phi = 1 - \Phi$. The attack fraction $\phi$
of Section~\ref{sec:foil} is a separate object and does not reappear after it.
Precisions are written $h$ and $\tau$, variances $v$ and $\hat V$, and
standard deviations $\sigma$ and $\varsigma$: so $\bar\tau$ and
$\tau_{\mathrm{status}}$ below are precisions, while $\sigma_\Delta$ and
$\sigma_D$ are standard deviations.

\section{The transfer channel cannot produce the result}
\label{sec:foil}

\subsection{The map}

\citet{nakaguma2025} model a campaign attack as a transfer. An attack from $k$
on $j$ moves a fraction $\phi$ of $j$'s support away from $j$; in their
Appendix G.2 demobilization extension a fraction $\alpha$ of that mass leaves
for the outside option instead of reaching $k$, and the attacker loses a
fraction $\gamma$ of its own support to backlash.

Their equilibrium has every candidate target the highest-ranked opponent, so
in a three-candidate race the profile is $1 \to 2$, $2 \to 1$ and $3 \to 1$:
mutual between the top two, with the third attacking the leader. Their data
agrees. Measuring attacks as electoral lawsuits, the observed frequencies in
three-candidate races are $2 \to 1$ at 3.0pp, $1 \to 2$ at 1.9pp and
$3 \to 1$ at 0.8pp. The second-place candidate is the most aggressive and the
front-runner is the most attacked.

\subsection{The contraction}

\begin{proposition}[Transfer cannot consolidate]
\label{prop:foil}
Take $\phi > 0$, $\alpha \le 1$, $\gamma \ge 0$ and the feasibility constraint
$\phi + \gamma \le 1$, which says a candidate cannot lose more support than it
has. Under mutual $1 \leftrightarrow 2$ attacks, post-attack support is
\[
  s_1 = s^\circ_1(1 - \phi - \gamma) + \phi(1-\alpha)\,s^\circ_2,
  \qquad
  s_2 = s^\circ_2(1 - \phi - \gamma) + \phi(1-\alpha)\,s^\circ_1,
\]
so the top-two margin satisfies
\[
  s_1 - s_2 \;=\; (s^\circ_1 - s^\circ_2)\,\kappa,
  \qquad
  \kappa \;\equiv\; 1 - \gamma - \phi(2-\alpha) \;<\; 1 .
\]
Adding $3 \to 1$ subtracts a further $\phi\,s^\circ_1$ from the margin. Hence
the transfer map cannot widen the top-two margin while preserving the identity
of the leader. The conclusion survives normalizing by the surviving valid
vote, and it survives letting the two attack intensities differ.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1 (the contraction).} Subtracting,
\[
  s_1 - s_2
  = (s^\circ_1 - s^\circ_2)(1 - \phi - \gamma)
    - \phi(1-\alpha)(s^\circ_1 - s^\circ_2)
  = (s^\circ_1 - s^\circ_2)\bigl[1 - \gamma - \phi(2-\alpha)\bigr].
\]
Since $\alpha \le 1$ we have $2 - \alpha \ge 1$, so $\phi(2-\alpha) \ge \phi >
0$, and with $\gamma \ge 0$ this gives $\kappa < 1$. If $\kappa \in (0,1)$ the
margin cannot widen: it narrows strictly when $s^\circ_1 > s^\circ_2$, and it
stays at zero when $s^\circ_1 = s^\circ_2$, which the weak ordering of
Section~\ref{sec:env} admits. If $\kappa \le 0$ the sign of $s_1 - s_2$ is
non-positive, so candidate 2 has caught or passed candidate 1 and the leader
is no longer the pre-attack leader. When $3 \to 1$ is added, candidate 1 loses
a further $\phi\,s^\circ_1$ and candidate 2 loses nothing, so
$s_1 - s_2 = (s^\circ_1 - s^\circ_2)\kappa - \phi\,s^\circ_1$, which is
smaller still.

\emph{Step 2 (shares, not levels).} The map leaks mass: a fraction $\alpha$ of
every transfer goes to the outside option and a fraction $\gamma$ of each
attacker's own support is lost to backlash. So $s_1 - s_2$ is a level margin
while the estimates are vote-share margins, and the claim has to be checked
after normalization. Write $S = s^\circ_1 + s^\circ_2$ and leave $s^\circ_3$
untouched. Then $s_1 + s_2 = S(1 - \gamma - \phi\alpha)$ and the surviving
total is $T = S(1 - \gamma - \phi\alpha) + s^\circ_3$. The normalized margin
widens only if $\kappa/T > 1/(S + s^\circ_3)$, that is only if
\[
  2\phi(\alpha - 1)\,S \;>\; s^\circ_3\,(1 - \kappa).
\]
The left side is at most zero because $\alpha \le 1$; the right side is at
least zero because $1 - \kappa = \gamma + \phi(2-\alpha) > 0$. The inequality
fails, so the normalized margin cannot widen either.

\emph{Step 3 (asymmetric intensities).} Let $\phi_{12}$ and $\phi_{21}$ be the
fractions the leader and the runner-up strip from each other. Then
\[
  s_1 - s_2 = (s^\circ_1 - s^\circ_2)(1-\gamma)
    - (2-\alpha)\bigl(\phi_{21}s^\circ_1 - \phi_{12}s^\circ_2\bigr),
\]
so widening requires $\phi_{12}/\phi_{21} > s^\circ_1/s^\circ_2 > 1$: the
leader would have to attack harder than the runner-up, by more than its own
support advantage. The documented intensities run the other way,
$\phi_{12}/\phi_{21} \approx 1.9/3.0$, so the asymmetric case holds a
fortiori and the closing claim covers the whole class.
\end{proof}

A grid check of the closed form and of the no-widening claim, including the
$3 \to 1$ profile, is in \texttt{docs/model/check\_proposition1.py}.

\begin{remark}
Widening the margin under the transfer map requires a one-sided downward
attack, $1 \to 2$ with no reciprocation. That is excluded by the equilibrium,
in which candidate 2's best target is candidate 1, and it is not what the data
shows. The magnitudes do not reach the result either. An attack frequency of
3pp means the transfer occurs with probability $0.03$, and conditional on
occurring it moves $\phi\,s^\circ$. Even at a generous $\phi = 0.3$ against a
target holding a third of the vote, the expected move is $0.03 \times 0.3
\times 0.33 \approx 0.003$: about three tenths of a point, an order of
magnitude below the three-point vote move being explained.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Object of comparison]
\citet{nakaguma2025} work with a win probability through a logit contest
function, whereas the estimates here are vote-share margins. The two are
monotone in $s_1 - s_2$, so Proposition~\ref{prop:foil} transfers, but the
mapping is an assumption and not an identity.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What Proposition~\ref{prop:foil} does not do]
\label{rem:orderstat}
The top-two margin is an order statistic, so any mean-preserving increase in
the dispersion of vote shares widens it mechanically.
Proposition~\ref{prop:foil} rules out the transfer channel under the
documented attack direction; it does not rule out a generic dispersion shock.
What rules that out is the ballot response and the contrast between seat
types in Section~\ref{sec:seat}: a dispersion shock predicts neither, and
predicts no directional flow from the runner-up to the leader.
\end{remark}

\section{What a filing emits}
\label{sec:signals}

A filing carries two signals, and their precisions differ by an order of
magnitude.

\paragraph{The type signal.}
A filing is an allegation about $\theta_j$. It is informative with probability
$\pi$ and pure noise with probability $1 - \pi$, and the voter cannot tell
which. Writing $\tau_I$ for the precision of an informative filing, the
effective precision is $\bar\tau = \pi\tau_I$; the race emits a public signal
$x_j = \theta_j + e_j$ with $e_j \sim N(0, 1/\bar\tau)$ independent across
candidates, and the posterior is normal with
\[
  \hat\theta_j \;=\; \frac{h_j m_j + \bar\tau x_j}{h_j + \bar\tau},
  \qquad
  \hat V(\theta_j) \;=\; \frac{1}{h_j + \bar\tau}.
\]
Genuine information about a candidate and a weapon that happens to persuade
enter through the same $\bar\tau$. They are therefore not separable for the
voter, and not separable for the econometrician either. The
non-separability that the empirical design has to live with is a property of
the environment, not a shortcoming of the instrument
\citep{feddersen1996}.

\paragraph{The status signal.}
The filer is optimizing and targets the highest-ranked opponent, which is the
premise Proposition~\ref{prop:foil} takes from \citet{nakaguma2025}. So the
identity of the target is informative about who is ahead, and its precision
$\tau_{\mathrm{status}}$ is high, because the filer knows the race. A court
case also draws local attention, and revealing which candidates are first and
second is known to move votes \citep{anagol2016}.

Litigation is thus precise about position and imprecise about quality.

\section{Consolidation}
\label{sec:consolidation}

\subsection{What \texorpdfstring{$\lambda$}{lambda} does to the primitives}

The modeling content of the whole note sits in one assumption, so it is stated
as one rather than smuggled into a proof.

\begin{assumption}[The noisy arena]
\label{as:arena}
$\dfrac{d\bar\tau}{d\lambda} < 0$ and
$\dfrac{d\tau_{\mathrm{status}}}{d\lambda} > 0$.
\end{assumption}

Judicialization crowds the arena with procedural content -- propaganda,
registration, campaign accounting -- that carries little information about
whether a candidate would govern well. Raising $\lambda$ therefore lowers
$\pi$, the chance that a given filing is about valence at all, and
$\bar\tau = \pi\tau_I$ falls with it. The same suits are public, costly, and
filed only against opponents worth attacking, so more of them sharpen common
knowledge of the rank order. One channel degrades information about
\emph{type}; the other improves information about \emph{rank}. That asymmetry
is the assumption, and the rest of this section is its consequences.

\subsection{The type channel}

\begin{lemma}[The posterior gap]
\label{lem:gap}
Restrict the top two to a common prior precision, $h_1 = h_2 = h$;
Proposition~\ref{prop:seat} relaxes it. Let the priors be independent across
candidates. Write $w = \bar\tau/(h + \bar\tau)$,
$\Delta = \hat\theta_1 - \hat\theta_2$, and $\varsigma^2 = 2v_\varepsilon$ for
the variance of $\varepsilon_{i1} - \varepsilon_{i2}$. Taken ex ante, over the
prior on $(\theta_1,\theta_2)$ as well as over the signal noise, $\Delta$ is
normal with
\[
  \mu_\Delta \;\equiv\; \mathbb{E}[\Delta] \;=\; m_1 - m_2,
  \qquad
  \sigma^2_\Delta \;\equiv\; \mathrm{Var}(\Delta)
  \;=\; \frac{2\bar\tau}{h\,(h + \bar\tau)} ,
\]
so $\mu_\Delta$ carries no $\bar\tau$, while
\[
  \frac{d\sigma^2_\Delta}{d\bar\tau}
  \;=\; \frac{2}{(h + \bar\tau)^2} \;>\; 0
  \qquad\text{for every } \bar\tau > 0 .
\]
Suppose $\mu_\Delta > 0$, that is, the leader also holds the better
reputation. Then
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] the probability of a rank reversal is
      $\Pr(\Delta < 0) = \Phi(-\mu_\Delta/\sigma_\Delta)$, strictly
      \emph{increasing} in $\sigma_\Delta$;
\item[(b)] the expected two-way margin between the top two is
      \[
        \mathbb{E}\bigl[\,2\Phi(\Delta/\varsigma) - 1\,\bigr]
        \;=\; 2\,\Phi\!\left(
          \frac{\mu_\Delta}{\sqrt{\varsigma^2 + \sigma^2_\Delta}}
        \right) - 1,
      \]
      strictly \emph{decreasing} in $\sigma_\Delta$.
\end{enumerate}
Under Assumption~\ref{as:arena}, $\sigma_\Delta$ is strictly decreasing in
$\lambda$. So a rise in $\lambda$ makes rank reversals rarer and the expected
two-way margin wider, with no restriction on $\bar\tau$ relative to $h$ and
nothing held fixed.
\end{lemma}

The frame is ex ante and that is the modeling content, not a technicality: the
comparative static is taken across realizations of the campaign, which is the
object the estimates correspond to -- a cross-section of municipalities that
differ in $\lambda$, not the single realized draw of one municipality.

\begin{proof}
With a common $h$, $\hat\theta_j = (1-w)m_j + w x_j$, where
$x_j = \theta_j + e_j$ with $\theta_j \sim N(m_j, 1/h)$ and
$e_j \sim N(0, 1/\bar\tau)$, all four draws independent. So $x_j$ is normal
with mean $m_j$ and variance $1/h + 1/\bar\tau$, giving
$\mathbb{E}[\hat\theta_j] = (1-w)m_j + w\,m_j = m_j$, which carries no
$\bar\tau$, and
\[
  \mathrm{Var}(\hat\theta_j)
  = w^2\left(\frac{1}{h} + \frac{1}{\bar\tau}\right)
  = \frac{\bar\tau^2}{(h+\bar\tau)^2}\cdot\frac{h+\bar\tau}{h\,\bar\tau}
  = \frac{\bar\tau}{h\,(h+\bar\tau)} .
\]
Since $\Delta$ is a linear combination of independent normals it is normal,
with $\mu_\Delta = m_1 - m_2$ and
$\sigma^2_\Delta = 2\bar\tau/[h(h+\bar\tau)]$. Differentiating,
\[
  \frac{d\sigma^2_\Delta}{d\bar\tau}
  = \frac{d}{d\bar\tau}\,\frac{2\bar\tau}{h^2 + h\bar\tau}
  = \frac{2(h^2 + h\bar\tau) - 2h\bar\tau}{(h^2 + h\bar\tau)^2}
  = \frac{2h^2}{h^2(h+\bar\tau)^2}
  = \frac{2}{(h+\bar\tau)^2},
\]
positive for every $\bar\tau > 0$. The two limits fix what the expression
means: $\sigma^2_\Delta \to 0$ as $\bar\tau \to 0$, where the posteriors never
leave the reputations, and $\sigma^2_\Delta \to 2/h$ as $\bar\tau \to \infty$,
where they reach the valences themselves.

(a) $\Delta \sim N(\mu_\Delta, \sigma^2_\Delta)$, so
$\Pr(\Delta<0) = \Phi(-\mu_\Delta/\sigma_\Delta)$ and
\[
  \frac{\partial \Pr(\Delta<0)}{\partial\sigma_\Delta}
  = \varphi\!\left(\frac{\mu_\Delta}{\sigma_\Delta}\right)
    \frac{\mu_\Delta}{\sigma^2_\Delta} \;>\; 0
  \qquad\text{whenever } \mu_\Delta > 0 .
\]

(b) Under the common-$h$ restriction the two ambiguity terms coincide and
cancel from the comparison between the top two, so voter $i$ prefers candidate
1 to candidate 2 exactly when $\Delta + \varepsilon_{i1} - \varepsilon_{i2} >
0$. Conditional on $\Delta$ the two-way margin is therefore
$2\Phi(\Delta/\varsigma) - 1$. Write $\Delta = \mu_\Delta + \sigma_\Delta Z$
with $Z$ standard normal and use
$\mathbb{E}[\Phi(a + bZ)] = \Phi\bigl(a/\sqrt{1+b^2}\bigr)$, which follows
from $\Phi(a+bZ) = \Pr(U - bZ \le a)$ for an independent standard normal $U$
together with $U - bZ \sim N(0, 1+b^2)$. With $a = \mu_\Delta/\varsigma$ and
$b = \sigma_\Delta/\varsigma$ the expectation is
$\Phi\bigl(\mu_\Delta/\sqrt{\varsigma^2+\sigma^2_\Delta}\bigr)$, strictly
decreasing in $\sigma_\Delta$ when $\mu_\Delta > 0$.

Finally $d\sigma_\Delta/d\lambda
= (d\sigma_\Delta/d\bar\tau)(d\bar\tau/d\lambda) < 0$: the first factor is
positive for every $\bar\tau > 0$ and the second is negative by
Assumption~\ref{as:arena}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why this is not a Jensen argument]
Part (b) is not obtained by calling the map from $\Delta$ to the margin
concave. That map is concave on the positive half-line and convex on the
negative one, so a mean-preserving contraction of a distribution with support
on both sides gets no unambiguous sign from curvature alone, and an argument
that took one would be assuming its conclusion. What delivers the sign is the
exact Gaussian convolution identity, which needs no support condition. The
reading is also sharper than a curvature argument would give: noise does not
favor the leader directly. It supplies the only route by which the runner-up
could have overtaken, and lowering it closes that route. Part (a) says the
same thing without any vote-share mapping at all.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Why the lemma is stated ex ante, and what is lost]
Conditioning on the realized valences instead gives
$\mu_\Delta = (1-w)(m_1 - m_2) + w(\theta_1 - \theta_2)$, which is a function
of $w$ and therefore of $\bar\tau$:
\[
  \frac{d\mu_\Delta}{d\bar\tau}
  \;=\; \frac{h}{(h+\bar\tau)^2}
        \bigl[(\theta_1 - \theta_2) - (m_1 - m_2)\bigr].
\]
Conditional on a leader whose true valence advantage exceeds its reputational
advantage, that derivative is positive, so a fall in $\bar\tau$ lowers
$\mu_\Delta$ and the mean channel runs \emph{against} the variance channel.
It can also dominate it: at $h = 1$, $v_\varepsilon = 1$,
$m_1 - m_2 = 0.01$ and $\theta_1 - \theta_2 = 1$, moving $\bar\tau$ from $0.5$
to $0.4$ raises the conditional reversal probability from $0.305$ to $0.323$
and lowers the conditional expected margin from $0.172$ to $0.150$ -- both
against Lemma~\ref{lem:gap}. So the conditional statement has no sign, and the
lemma is not merely more convenient stated ex ante: it is only true stated ex
ante. What is lost is any claim about a single race whose valences are already
drawn; what is kept is the claim about the cross-section, which is what the
estimates measure.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The coordination channel}

The second leg is a standard result and is cited here rather than re-derived.
As $\tau_{\mathrm{status}}$ rises the rank order approaches common
knowledge, and in a coordination equilibrium voters desert candidates ranked
third and below, with the desertion running toward the believed leader
\citep{myerson1993,fey1997,cox1997}. We import it in reduced form: a fraction
$g(\tau_{\mathrm{status}}) \in [0,1]$ of the voters attached to candidates
ranked third and below deserts, with $g' > 0$, and a fraction $q > 1/2$ of
those deserters moves to the believed leader.

\subsection{The proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Consolidation]
\label{prop:consolidation}
Let $n \in [0,1)$ be the mass of voters attached to candidates ranked third
and below. Under Assumptions~\ref{as:impressions} and~\ref{as:arena}, with
$h_1 = h_2 = h$ and $\mu_\Delta > 0$, the expected top-two margin is
\[
  \mathbb{E}\,[\,s_1 - s_2\,]
  \;=\; (1-n)\left[\,2\,\Phi\!\left(
      \frac{\mu_\Delta}{\sqrt{\varsigma^2 + \sigma^2_\Delta}}\right) - 1
      \,\right]
   \;+\; n\,g(\tau_{\mathrm{status}})\,(2q-1),
\]
and
\[
  \frac{d\,\mathbb{E}[\,s_1 - s_2\,]}{d\lambda} \;>\; 0 .
\]
The probability of a rank reversal falls, so the identity of the leader is
more likely to be preserved rather than less, and the size of the field is
unchanged.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The mass $1-n$ attached to the top two splits between them, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:gap}(b) its expected contribution to the margin is
$(1-n)\bigl[2\Phi(\mu_\Delta/\sqrt{\varsigma^2+\sigma^2_\Delta}) - 1\bigr]$.
Of the mass $n$ attached to the rest, a fraction $g$ deserts, $q$ of it to
candidate 1 and $1-q$ to candidate 2, contributing
$n\,g\,\bigl(q - (1-q)\bigr) = n\,g\,(2q-1)$. Adding gives the display.

Differentiate. The first term moves through $\sigma_\Delta$ alone, because by
Lemma~\ref{lem:gap} the ex ante $\mu_\Delta$ equals $m_1 - m_2$ and so carries
no $\bar\tau$ and no $\lambda$; and
\[
  \frac{\partial}{\partial \sigma_\Delta}
  \left[2\Phi\!\left(\frac{\mu_\Delta}
      {\sqrt{\varsigma^2+\sigma^2_\Delta}}\right)-1\right]
  \cdot \frac{d\sigma_\Delta}{d\lambda} \;>\; 0,
\]
because the bracket is strictly decreasing in $\sigma_\Delta$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:gap}(b) while $d\sigma_\Delta/d\lambda < 0$ by
Assumption~\ref{as:arena}. The second term has derivative
$n(2q-1)\,g'(\tau_{\mathrm{status}})\,
 d\tau_{\mathrm{status}}/d\lambda \ge 0$, since $q > 1/2$, $g' > 0$ and
$d\tau_{\mathrm{status}}/d\lambda > 0$ by Assumption~\ref{as:arena}, and it
is strictly positive when $n > 0$. Both channels are non-negative and the
first is strictly positive, so the sum is. The reversal statement is
Lemma~\ref{lem:gap}(a) with $\sigma_\Delta$ falling in $\lambda$. The field is
fixed because registration closes before the filings (Section~\ref{sec:env}).
\end{proof}

The candidate-supply null is structural rather than incidental: the status
signal arrives after the registration deadline, so it cannot act on the
candidate set.

\begin{proposition}[Concentration indices are redundant]
\label{prop:concentration}
Write $p_1$ and $p_2$ for the observed shares of the leader and the runner-up,
and let the shift be a one-parameter reallocation between them:
$p_1 \mapsto p_1 + \eta$ and $p_2 \mapsto p_2 - \eta$, with every other
candidate's share unchanged. Then
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] the combined top-two share $p_1 + p_2$ is \emph{invariant} in
      $\eta$;
\item[(ii)] $\mathrm{HHI}(\eta) = \mathrm{HHI}(0) + 2\eta(p_1-p_2) + 2\eta^2$,
      so $d\mathrm{HHI}/d\eta = 2(p_1 - p_2 + 2\eta)$, which changes sign at
      $\eta = -(p_1-p_2)/2$; the index is U-shaped in $\eta$ and monotone only
      on $\eta \ge -(p_1-p_2)/2$, which is exactly the region where the leader
      stays ahead;
\item[(iii)] on that region the effective number of candidates
      $1/\mathrm{HHI}$ is strictly decreasing in $\eta$.
\end{enumerate}
The margin itself is $(p_1-p_2) + 2\eta$. So on the region where the leader
stays ahead each index is a deterministic function of the same single
parameter, and none of them carries a restriction independent of the margin.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) $(p_1+\eta) + (p_2-\eta) = p_1 + p_2$ for every $\eta$.

(ii) Expanding, $(p_1+\eta)^2 + (p_2-\eta)^2
= p_1^2 + p_2^2 + 2\eta(p_1-p_2) + 2\eta^2$; the other candidates' shares
enter $\mathrm{HHI}$ additively and are unchanged. Differentiating gives
$2(p_1-p_2) + 4\eta$, which vanishes at $\eta = -(p_1-p_2)/2$ and is positive
above it. The leader stays ahead when $p_1 + \eta \ge p_2 - \eta$, that is when
$\eta \ge -(p_1-p_2)/2$: the same threshold.

(iii) $x \mapsto 1/x$ is strictly decreasing on $x > 0$, and $\mathrm{HHI}$ is
strictly increasing in $\eta$ there. Finally
$(p_1+\eta) - (p_2-\eta) = (p_1-p_2) + 2\eta$.
\end{proof}

This is why concentration cannot identify which mechanism is at work. The
point is not that the concentration estimates are imprecise; it is that even a
precise one would be the margin restated -- and in the case of the combined
top-two share it would carry no information at all. Pushing the estimated
top-two reallocation through each municipality's own 2024 share vector implies
$\Delta\mathrm{HHI} = +0.018$ and $\Delta\mathrm{ENP} = -0.055$, while the
separately estimated index regressions give $+0.023$ (0.013) and $-0.044$
(0.039). Both implied values sit inside the estimated intervals, so the index
regressions add no restriction to the margin result
(\texttt{docs/model/check\_proposition3.py}).

\section{Exit and the seat split}
\label{sec:seat}

\subsection{Exit}

\begin{proposition}[Exit]
\label{prop:exit}
Once the status signal makes the rank order common knowledge the pivot
probability collapses and the choice becomes expressive: voter $i$ marks the
candidate solving
\[
  \max_j \;\Bigl[\,\hat\theta_j - \frac{\rho/2}{h_j + \bar\tau}
    + \varepsilon_{ij}\,\Bigr]
\]
if that maximum is at least $\bar b$, and marks blank otherwise. Write
$c_j = \bar b - \hat\theta_j + (\rho/2)/(h_j+\bar\tau)$. Holding the posterior
means fixed,
\[
  \frac{d\Pr(\mathrm{blank})}{d\lambda}
  \;=\; -\,\frac{d\bar\tau}{d\lambda}
    \sum_j \frac{\rho/2}{(h_j+\bar\tau)^2}\,
    \frac{\varphi\bigl(c_j/\sqrt{v_\varepsilon}\bigr)}{\sqrt{v_\varepsilon}}
    \prod_{k \ne j}\Phi\bigl(c_k/\sqrt{v_\varepsilon}\bigr)
  \;>\; 0
\]
under Assumption~\ref{as:arena}, and the effect is exactly zero at $\rho = 0$.
Under compulsory voting the exit option is a blank or null vote rather than
abstention, so turnout is unmoved.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The voter blanks when every candidate falls short of $\bar b$, that is when
$\varepsilon_{ij} < c_j$ for all $j$. The $\varepsilon_{ij}$ are independent
$N(0,v_\varepsilon)$, so
$\Pr(\mathrm{blank}) = \prod_j \Phi\bigl(c_j/\sqrt{v_\varepsilon}\bigr)$.
Holding $\hat\theta_j$ fixed,
$\partial c_j/\partial\bar\tau = -(\rho/2)(h_j+\bar\tau)^{-2} \le 0$, so
\[
  \frac{\partial \Pr(\mathrm{blank})}{\partial \bar\tau}
  = \sum_j
    \frac{\varphi\bigl(c_j/\sqrt{v_\varepsilon}\bigr)}{\sqrt{v_\varepsilon}}
    \frac{\partial c_j}{\partial \bar\tau}
    \prod_{k\ne j}\Phi\bigl(c_k/\sqrt{v_\varepsilon}\bigr) \;\le\; 0,
\]
strictly negative when $\rho > 0$ and identically zero when $\rho = 0$.
Multiplying by $d\bar\tau/d\lambda < 0$ gives the display and its sign.
Conditioning on the posterior means is what isolates the ambiguity channel;
their own movement in $\bar\tau$ is the type channel, which
Proposition~\ref{prop:consolidation} already treats, and it leaves
$\mathbb{E}[\hat\theta_j] = m_j$ for every $\bar\tau$.
\end{proof}

The mechanism is the posterior variance. $\hat V(\theta_j) = 1/(h_j +
\bar\tau)$ is decreasing in $\bar\tau$, so under Assumption~\ref{as:arena} a
rise in $\lambda$ raises the posterior variance of \emph{every} candidate's
valence, and an ambiguity-averse voter discounts every candidate by more. The
maximum falls, more voters find no name worth $\bar b$, and the blank rate
rises. The blank response and the consolidation are then the same single
parameter movement seen from two sides -- $\bar\tau$ falling widens the margin
through Lemma~\ref{lem:gap} and empties the ballot through
Proposition~\ref{prop:exit}. Because the effect vanishes at $\rho = 0$, the
blank rise is the testable content of ambiguity aversion rather than a
restatement of it \citep{ghirardato2006}; and compulsory voting is what routes
the response into the mark rather than into attendance, which is the margin
the abstention literature works on \citep{feddersen1996}.

\subsection{The seat split}

The seat dimension enters through one primitive, the prior precision on the
leader's valence. In a contested seat an incumbent is running, so $h_1$ is
high: voters have already learned $\theta_1$ and have already sorted on it. In
an open seat there is no incumbent, so $h_1$ is low and there is nothing to
have sorted on.

\begin{lemma}[Selection among the runner-up's supporters]
\label{lem:selection}
Under Assumption~\ref{as:impressions}, voter $i$ starts out supporting
candidate 2 exactly when
\[
  D_i \;\equiv\; (\varepsilon_{i1} + \zeta_{i1})
    - (\varepsilon_{i2} + \zeta_{i2}) \;<\; -\delta,
  \qquad
  \delta \;\equiv\; \Bigl(m_1 - \tfrac{\rho/2}{h_1}\Bigr)
    - \Bigl(m_2 - \tfrac{\rho/2}{h_2}\Bigr),
\]
and $\delta \ge 0$ is equivalent to $s^\circ_1 \ge s^\circ_2$. Write
$\sigma_D^2 = 2v_\varepsilon + \kappa_\zeta/h_1 + \kappa_\zeta/h_2$,
$z = \delta/\sigma_D$ and
$r = \sqrt{v_\varepsilon}/\sigma_D = \mathrm{corr}(\varepsilon_{i1}, D_i)$.
Then
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $\displaystyle
      \mathbb{E}\bigl[\varepsilon_{i1} \mid D_i < -\delta\bigr]
      = -\,\frac{v_\varepsilon}{\sigma_D}\,\frac{\varphi(z)}{\Phi(-z)}$,
      which is strictly increasing in $\sigma_D$ and hence strictly
      \emph{decreasing} in $h_1$;
\item[(b)] for every threshold $t$,
      $\Pr\bigl(\varepsilon_{i1} \ge t \mid D_i < -\delta\bigr)$ is strictly
      decreasing in $h_1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$D_i$ is symmetric about zero, so $s^\circ_1 = \Pr(D_i \ge -\delta) \ge 1/2$
exactly when $\delta \ge 0$; that is the labeling of Section~\ref{sec:env} and
not an extra hypothesis.

(a) $(\varepsilon_{i1}, D_i)$ is bivariate normal with
$\mathrm{Cov}(\varepsilon_{i1}, D_i) = v_\varepsilon$, so
$\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_{i1} \mid D_i] = (v_\varepsilon/\sigma_D^2)\,D_i$ and
\[
  \mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_{i1} \mid D_i < -\delta]
  = \frac{v_\varepsilon}{\sigma_D^2}\,
    \mathbb{E}[D_i \mid D_i < -\delta]
  = \frac{v_\varepsilon}{\sigma_D^2}
    \left(-\sigma_D\frac{\varphi(z)}{\Phi(-z)}\right)
  = -\frac{v_\varepsilon}{\sigma_D}\,M(z),
\]
where $M(z) = \varphi(z)/\Phi(-z)$ is the standard normal hazard rate, which
is positive and strictly increasing. Raising $\sigma_D$ lowers
$v_\varepsilon/\sigma_D$ and, because $\delta \ge 0$, lowers $z$ and so does
not raise $M(z)$; the product falls and its negative rises. And $\sigma_D$ is
strictly decreasing in $h_1$.

(b) Standardize. With $a = t/\sqrt{v_\varepsilon}$ the quantity is
$\Pr(Z_1 \ge a \mid Z_2 \le -z)$ for a standard bivariate normal pair with
correlation $r > 0$, and it is decreasing in each of $z$ and $r$. In $z$:
\[
  \Pr(Z_1 \ge a \mid Z_2 \le -z)
  = \mathbb{E}\left[\left.\bar\Phi\!\left(\frac{a - rZ_2}{\sqrt{1-r^2}}\right)
    \;\right|\; Z_2 \le -z\right],
\]
whose integrand is increasing in $Z_2$ because $r > 0$, while the conditional
law of $Z_2$ given $Z_2 \le -z$ falls in the first-order stochastic order as
$z$ rises. In $r$: by Plackett's identity,
$\partial\Pr(Z_1 \le a_1, Z_2 \le a_2)/\partial r$ equals the bivariate normal
density at $(a_1,a_2)$ and is therefore positive; applying it to
$\Pr(-Z_1 \le -a,\; Z_2 \le -z)$, whose pair has correlation $-r$, makes the
numerator decreasing in $r$, while the denominator $\Phi(-z)$ does not depend
on $r$. Raising $h_1$ lowers $\sigma_D$, which raises both $z$ and $r$, so it
lowers the conditional probability.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The seat split]
\label{prop:seat}
Take the runner-up's departing supporters, the mass that
Proposition~\ref{prop:consolidation} moves. Each of them either marks
candidate 1 or marks blank, by the rule of Proposition~\ref{prop:exit}. At a
common threshold -- in particular at $\rho = 0$ -- the fraction that marks
candidate 1 is strictly lower where the seat is contested. In that case the
runner-up loses a comparable mass in either kind of seat, and what differs is
where that mass goes: to the winner in an open seat, with the valid vote
holding, and split between the winner and the blank ballot in a contested
seat.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
First, who departs. Proposition~\ref{prop:consolidation}'s display allocates
the top-two mass by a probit rule on $\Delta + \varepsilon_{i1} -
\varepsilon_{i2}$ and never names a departing set, so the set is named here:
the voters who leave the runner-up are candidate 2's camp
$\{i : D_i < -\delta\}$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:selection}, or a uniform random
subsample of it, which is what makes the conditional law of
$\varepsilon_{i1}$ among them the law that lemma computes. That is an
assumption about who departs, not a consequence of the display.

A departing supporter of candidate 2 marks candidate 1 when
$\hat\theta_1 - (\rho/2)/(h_1+\bar\tau) + \varepsilon_{i1} \ge \bar b$, that
is when $\varepsilon_{i1} \ge t$ with
$t = \bar b - \hat\theta_1 + (\rho/2)/(h_1 + \bar\tau)$, and blanks otherwise.
The fraction marking candidate 1 is therefore
$\Pr(\varepsilon_{i1} \ge t \mid D_i < -\delta)$, which at any fixed $t$ is
strictly decreasing in $h_1$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:selection}(b). A contested seat
is a high-$h_1$ seat and an open seat a low-$h_1$ seat, which gives the
ordering.

For that to be the whole of the effect the threshold has to be the same in the
two seats, and at $\rho = 0$ it is not automatically: $t$ reduces to
$\bar b - \hat\theta_1$, which still carries $h_1$ through
$\hat\theta_1 = (h_1 m_1 + \bar\tau x_1)/(h_1 + \bar\tau)$, running from $x_1$
toward $m_1$ as $h_1$ grows. The ex ante frame of Lemma~\ref{lem:gap} removes
it: $\mathbb{E}[\hat\theta_1] = m_1$ for every $h_1$, so across realizations
the $\rho = 0$ threshold is $\bar b - m_1$ in both seat types and the
comparison is exactly Lemma~\ref{lem:selection}(b). That is the same device
Proposition~\ref{prop:exit}'s proof uses, for the same reason.
\end{proof}

The split comes from selection on $\varepsilon$ alone, and it is worth being
explicit about what the ambiguity term of Proposition~\ref{prop:exit} does
here, because it does not generate the split and does not even help. That term
raises the blank rate in \emph{both} kinds of seat, so on its own it predicts
no contrast. Worse, its level differs in the unhelpful direction:
$\hat V(\theta_1) = 1/(h_1 + \bar\tau)$ is largest exactly where $h_1$ is
smallest, so the ambiguity penalty on the leader is heaviest in the open seat,
which is where the model predicts the least blanking. Formally, $t$ is higher
in the open seat when $\rho > 0$. The prediction therefore holds outright at
$\rho = 0$ and, for $\rho > 0$, requires the selection effect of
Lemma~\ref{lem:selection}(b) to dominate that differential penalty. Saying so
is the honest statement of it.

The reading is the one the mechanism suggests. Where the leader is a sitting
mayor, still supporting the challenger is a revealed rejection of someone the
whole electorate has had four years to judge, so those voters are selected on
genuinely low taste for the incumbent, and a large share of them will not mark
that name under any amount of coordination pressure. Where the leader is
fresh, starting out with the runner-up says much less, because the
pre-campaign split was driven by impressions that the campaign then washes
out; those voters' tastes are close to unselected, and most of them follow the
coordination.

\section{What the model does not do}
\label{sec:scope}

The model produces signs and orderings. It is not calibrated and not
structurally estimated. It does not separate a genuine allegation from a
persuasive weapon, and Section~\ref{sec:signals} is the reason that is a
property of the environment rather than a gap to be closed. It does not
endogenize the filer's decision: the attack direction is taken from
\citet{nakaguma2025} as a maintained assumption, because endogenizing it would
require exactly the selection the instrument exists to sidestep. It says
nothing about judges, and nothing about welfare. Runoff municipalities, where
a second round changes the coordination problem, are outside it.

Four of its steps are assumed rather than derived, and it is better to name
them than to let them read as results. Assumption~\ref{as:arena} is the whole
modeling content of Section~\ref{sec:consolidation}: nothing here shows that
$\lambda$ must push $\bar\tau$ down and $\tau_{\mathrm{status}}$ up, only
that everything else follows if it does. The second half of
Assumption~\ref{as:impressions} -- that pre-campaign impressions decide the
initial camps but are washed out of the ballot-stage payoff -- is what makes
the seat split run in the predicted direction; without it the selected object
in Lemma~\ref{lem:selection} would be the composite
$\varepsilon_{i1} + \zeta_{i1}$ rather than $\varepsilon_{i1}$, and the
selection would reverse, because a fresh leader's more dispersed impressions
would themselves make the runner-up's camp more extreme. The identity of the
departing set is assumed too, in Proposition~\ref{prop:seat}'s proof:
Proposition~\ref{prop:consolidation} moves mass between the top two without
naming who moves, so taking the movers to be candidate 2's camp, or a uniform
subsample of it, is what lets Lemma~\ref{lem:selection} apply to them. And the
coordination leg of Proposition~\ref{prop:consolidation} is imported from the
literature rather than derived \citep{myerson1993,fey1997,cox1997}.

One further limit is a matter of what the statements are about rather than of
what is assumed. Lemma~\ref{lem:gap} and everything downstream of it are ex
ante, taken over the prior on valences as well as over the signal noise, which
matches a cross-section of municipalities differing in $\lambda$ but says
nothing about a race whose valences are already drawn. Conditional on those,
the remark \emph{Why the lemma is stated ex ante} shows the two comparative
statics have no sign at all.

Two conditions bound the mechanism. Coordination needs a small number of
focal contenders, so a genuinely three-way race weakens it. And the reasoning
in Section~\ref{sec:consolidation} applies to a single-winner plurality
contest; an open-list proportional council race offers no focal rank for the
status signal to reveal, which is the model's clearest out-of-sample
implication.
