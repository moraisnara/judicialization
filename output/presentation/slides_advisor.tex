\begin{frame}{The Question: Does the Judicial Arena Level the Contest or Barricade It?}
\hypertarget{src:logic}{}
\footnotesize
\textbf{\textcolor{myblue}{Question.}} Does judicialization, the judiciary's \textbf{expanded role}, change who runs, how voters respond, and how open the contest stays?
\smallskip

\textbf{\textcolor{myblue}{What we tackle.}} A causal estimate: the $2020\!\to\!2024$ change in a municipality's adversarial litigation, instrumented by shift-share exposure to national topic-level diffusion, traced through the three layers below --- for the single-winner \emph{mayoral} race and, as the specificity check, the proportional \emph{council} race.
\smallskip

\textbf{\textcolor{myblue}{Hypotheses.}} Two readings of the same arena --- \pos{a leveller} that enforces the rules and widens access, \negt{a barrier} that raises the cost of competing --- disagreeing on \emph{sign} at every layer:
\smallskip

\begin{frame}{Layer 1 --- Who Runs: the Candidate Engine Is Quiet}
\hypertarget{src:candidates}{}
\small
If either reading operated through the field --- \pos{H1} widening the pool, \negt{H2} thinning it to fewer, safer names --- it would show here. It does not. Across both offices --- and across field size, gender, race, education, entry, and incumbency --- every candidate-supply margin is a null, as is the vote-weighted composition of \emph{winners}. Entry is an anticipation margin: candidacies register at the August deadline, before most suits resolve.
\smallskip
\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth,height=0.42\textheight,keepaspectratio]{../figures/candidate_supply_coefplot.pdf}
\end{center}
\smallskip
\takeaway{\textbf{Layer-1 verdict: neither hypothesis.} No supply margin moves, and neither does who \emph{wins} (female vote share $\FemaleVSPerSDSigned$~pp, n.s.) --- not \pos{H1's broader pool}, not \negt{H2's thinner field}. Whatever the arena does, it does not do it through candidates --- the action must be downstream, in voters and the result.}
\smallskip
\end{frame}


\begin{frame}{Layer 2 --- How Voters Respond: Withdrawal Where the Seat Is Defended}
\hypertarget{src:seathet}{}
\small
Turnout is \textbf{compulsory}, so withdrawal shows up \emph{inside} the ballot: \textbf{blank} and \textbf{null} votes that elect no one, and a falling \textbf{valid} share. Pooled across all mayoral races it barely moves --- splitting by \textbf{open} seat ($N=\OpenN$) versus \textbf{contested}, where the sitting mayor may run again ($N=\ContN$), shows why.
\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth,height=0.41\textheight,keepaspectratio]{../figures/voterbehavior_seat_coefplot.pdf}
\end{center}
\takeaway{\textbf{Layer-2 verdict: \negt{H2}, but only where a seat is defended.} Voters do not \pos{stay engaged}: where the sitting mayor may run again, blanks rise ($\ContBlankCoef$, $p=\ContBlankP$) and the valid share falls --- the \negt{withdrawal} signature. Where the seat is \textbf{open}, nothing moves ($p=\OpenBlankP$): the response is to a \emph{defended} seat, not to litigation as such. Tentative tier: clears conventional and exposure-robust inference ($p=\ERBlankPbhj$), not the AR bootstrap ($p=\ARBlankP$).}
\hfill\hyperlink{bk:seathet}{\beamergotobutton{full table}}
\end{frame}


\begin{frame}{Layer 3 --- How Open It Stays: the Mayoral Race Consolidates}
\hypertarget{src:spine1}{}
\small
The winner's vote share rises, the runner-up's falls, and the top-two margin between them widens --- the race \negt{consolidates}. Either face can produce that, so the reading comes from Layer 2's ballot. The proportional council race shows none of it.
\smallskip
\begin{center}
\scriptsize
\resizebox{\linewidth}{!}{\input{../tables/tex/executive_iv_competition.tex}}
\end{center}
\smallskip
\takeaway{\textbf{Layer-3 verdict: the consolidation, at full confidence.} A one-SD rise widens the winner's margin by $+\MarginPerSD$~pp ($p=\MarginP$), clearing the AR bootstrap ($p=\ARMarginP$) and the exposure-robust CI ($\ERMarginCI$) --- and it is general across seat types. The front-runner it reinforces is the \textbf{sitting incumbent} (${\approx}5\times$ the runner-up), a demographic near-twin --- which is why representation cannot move. The \negt{barrier} reading of it inherits Layer 2's tentative tier.}
\smallskip
\end{frame}


\begin{frame}{Layer 3 by Gender: an Evenly Shared Squeeze, a Male-Tilted Gain}
\hypertarget{src:gender}{}
\small
Layer 3 moves vote from the runner-up's slot to the winner's. Splitting both slots by the candidate's gender asks who pays for that transfer and who collects it. The split is \textbf{unconditional} --- components sum back to the layer-3 totals on the same sample, with no conditioning on who placed where.
\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth,height=0.39\textheight,keepaspectratio]{../figures/gender_consolidation_coefplot.pdf}
\end{center}
\takeaway{\textbf{Layer-3 incidence: the cost is gender-neutral; the gain may not be.} The runner-up's loss splits evenly (female $\FemRUSharePerSDSigned$~pp, male $\MaleRUSharePerSDSigned$~pp) --- that slot's gap is a \emph{precise} zero ($p=\RUGapP$). The gain lands on \textbf{male} front-runners ($\MaleWinSharePerSDSigned$~pp, $p=\MaleWinShareP$) while the female winner's share does not move ($p=\FemWinShareP$) --- but that slot's gap test is $\WinGapPerSDSigned$~pp, $p=\WinGapP$: \negt{suggestive, not established}. Net, women's share of the top two drifts down $\FemTopTwoPerSDSigned$~pp ($p=\FemTopTwoP$).}
\hfill\hyperlink{bk:gender}{\beamergotobutton{full table}}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Backup --- Who the Consolidation Favors: the Incumbent, Not a Demographic}
\hypertarget{bk:favored}{}
\small
Profiling the 2024 mayoral field by within-race vote rank, descriptively, before any regression: the winner and the runner-up part on \textbf{political standing}, not demographics. The front-runner is a sitting incumbent roughly five times as often; on gender, race, education, and age the top-two are near-twins.
\smallskip

\smallskip
\takeaway{Because the top-two are demographically interchangeable, widening the margin between them entrenches \textbf{incumbency} --- a neutral trait --- which is mechanically why the vote-weighted gender / race composition cannot move. \ns{Descriptive shares by vote rank; no causal content.}}
