
\begin{frame}{The Consolidation Measures Are Pre-Trend Clean}
\hypertarget{src:pretrend}{}
\small
A free 2016 lag raises a fair question: does the instrument load on municipalities already moving in the consolidating direction? To test this we regress the 2016$\to$2020 pre-period change on the instrument, conditioning only on pre-determined (2010 Census) covariates and state FE. A clean, share-exogenous design wants every coefficient near zero, since the shock should not predict past movement.

\footnotesize Every consolidation measure is clean. Margin ($p=\PtMarginP$), effective-N ($p=\PtEffNP$), Herfindahl ($p=\PtHHIP$) and top-two share ($p=\PtTopTwoP$) all straddle zero, as does ballot composition. One caveat is worth stating: turnout does pre-trend ($p<.01$), reflecting the convergence the first stage is identified off, and the valid share, being an electorate share, mechanically inherits it. Consolidation and ballot composition are untouched.
