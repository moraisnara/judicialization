\begin{frame}{Candidate Supply Does Not Move, in Either Office}
\hypertarget{src:candidates}{}
\small
Litigation could thin the field as a \negt{barrier}, since suits are costly to mount and defend, or open it as a weapon, since a credible threat of challenge can deter a front-runner from standing. The sign is therefore ambiguous \emph{a priori}. Entry is also an anticipation margin: candidacies register at the August deadline, before most suits resolve. Neither force shows up. Across both offices, and across field size, gender, race, education, entry and incumbency, every candidate-supply margin is a null.
\smallskip

\smallskip
\takeaway{No supply margin moves in either office: mayoral field size ($\CandExecCoef$, $p=\CandExecP$), its female ($\FemaleCandCoef$, $p=\FemaleCandP$), non-white, higher-ed, new-entrant and incumbent shares, and the council pool ($\LegCandCoef$, $p=\LegCandP$) all straddle zero. Mean age is flat in the mayoral field (\MayorAgeYr~yr); the council pool skews marginally younger (\CouncilAgeYr~yr, $p=\CouncilAgeP$), a lone wrinkle that fails multiple testing. What reshapes the race is therefore voter behavior, not the composition of the field.}\hfill\hyperlink{app:compbinary}{\beamergotobutton{count table}}
\end{frame}
