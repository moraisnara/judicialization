\begin{frame}{Related Literature}
\hypertarget{src:literature}{}
\footnotesize
Four literatures each name a piece of the phenomenon, but none delivers an aggregate causal estimate of what litigation does to a race. The paper's contribution is located against each in turn.
\begin{itemize}\itemsep4pt
  \item \emph{Judicialization of competition.} A comparative literature documents how courts come to settle the terms of political competition \ns{\citep{tate1995,hirschl2008,sieder2020}}, and in Brazil how the TSE rewrites electoral rules through adjudication \ns{\citep{marchetticortez2009}}. This work is descriptive; we estimate the effect on the contest.

  \item \emph{Electoral governance.} A second literature studies the fusion of rule-making, application and adjudication within a single branch as a problem of institutional design \ns{\citep{mozaffar2002,marchetti2008,marchetti2012}}. We instead treat litigation intensity as a shock that candidates and voters respond to.

  \item \emph{Lawsuits as strategy versus accountability.} A third body of work reads litigation either as a strategic weapon aimed at rivals \ns{\citep{chin2025,nakaguma2025}} or as a channel through which judicial decisions convey information \ns{\citep{assumpcao2019}}, with campaign money as a driver \ns{\citep{mancuso2023}}. These designs are candidate-level, the outcome being the candidate's own vote share, and the one direct look at voters bounds the polling effect near zero \ns{\citep{chin2025}}. We estimate the effect on the race itself.

  \item \emph{Shift-share identification.} A methodological literature develops topic-based Bartik instruments from leave-one-out flows interacted with pre-period shares \ns{\citep{ash2025,goldsmith2020,borusyak2022,autor2013}}. These designs are built for many diffuse shocks; we adapt the framework to the few, concentrated shocks characteristic of litigation data, relying on the share-exogeneity (GPS) route.
\end{itemize}
\vfill
{\footnotesize\centering The first three literatures characterize the phenomenon; the fourth allows us to identify it, giving an aggregate causal estimate of litigation's effect on the shape of the contest.\par}
\end{frame}
