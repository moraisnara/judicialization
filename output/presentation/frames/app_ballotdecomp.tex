The seat table mixes denominators: the top two as shares of valid votes, the
ballot as shares of registered voters. On one denominator the top two move by
similar amounts and what differs between seat types is the size of the valid
pie.

The runner-up's share of the electorate falls by a similar amount in either
kind of seat. Where the seat is contested the winner takes up only
\DecompContWinner\,pp of it: the blank vote rises (\ContBlankCoef,
$p=\ContBlankP$) as the valid vote falls (\ContValidCoef, $p=\ContValidP$).
Where the seat is open the winner takes up \DecompOpenWinner\,pp and the valid
vote holds (\OpenValidCoef,
$p=\OpenValidP$).\hfill\hyperlink{app:transferfoil}{\beamerreturnbutton{Back}}

{\centering\scriptsize\ns{Arithmetic on separate point estimates with no
propagated standard errors: an implication, not a result. Each $p$ is an
underlying coefficient's, not the cell's: the vote cells mix a share
coefficient with the valid-rate coefficient and show the share's $p$;
Blank/null sums blank and null and shows the smaller of the two $p$'s, a
selection over dependent tests, not a joint test; only Turnout's $p$ is its
own. The three valid shares should sum to zero and depart by
\DecompContResid\,pp (contested) and \DecompOpenResid\,pp (open), so the
others column is not interpretable. Only the contested row clears weak-IV
inference; the open row also fails its pre-trend (2SLS placebo on the
2016--2020 margin change, $p=\OpenPtMarginP$ against $p=\ContPtMarginP$),
while the blank rate carries the wedge because its reduced-form pre-trend on
the instrument passes ($p=\PtRFBlankP$) and the valid rate's does not
($p=\PtRFValidP$).}\par}
