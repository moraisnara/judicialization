
\begin{frame}{Appendix --- What a Filing Reveals}
\hypertarget{app:theory}{}
\footnotesize
A filing carries two signals, and their precisions differ. Voters are Bayesian
over a candidate's valence $\theta_j$, with prior $N(m_j, 1/h_j)$: $m_j$ is
reputation and $h_j$ is how settled the record is. Direct judicial action,
where a ruling removes a candidate and mechanically reallocates the vote, lies
outside the estimand: the adversarial filter drops the candidacy-registration
and campaign-finance machinery, so it is not what these estimates measure.
\smallskip
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.49\textwidth}
  \begin{block}{Type signal --- low precision}
  \scriptsize
  The filing is an allegation: informative with probability $\pi$, noise with
  probability $1-\pi$, and the voter cannot tell which. Effective precision is
  $\bar\tau = \pi\tau_I$, and
  $\hat\theta_j = (h_j m_j + \bar\tau x_j)/(h_j + \bar\tau)$.
  \hfill\ns{\citep{assumpcao2019,feddersen1996}}
  \end{block}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.49\textwidth}
  \begin{block}{Status signal --- high precision}
  \scriptsize
  The filer is optimizing and targets the highest-ranked opponent, so being
  sued identifies the front-runner. A court case also draws local attention to
  the race.
  \hfill\ns{\citep{chin2025,nakaguma2025,anagol2016}}
  \end{block}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\smallskip

Litigation is precise about position and imprecise about quality. An
accountability signal and a weapon that persuades enter the posterior through
the same $\bar\tau$, so they are not separable for the voter and not separable
here. What the design separates is whether litigation moves votes toward
someone or degrades the contest without re-sorting.
