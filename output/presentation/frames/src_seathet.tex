\begin{frame}{Which Face, by Seat Type}
\hypertarget{src:seathet}{}
\small
Mayoral races split by whether the seat is open, meaning the incumbent is term-limited so no front-runner defends it ($N=$~\OpenN{}), or contested, where the sitting mayor may still seek re-election ($N=$~\ContN{}). This split is what assigns the face. The margin widens in both, so only the ballot separates them.
\smallskip
\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth,height=0.34\textheight,keepaspectratio]{../figures/heterogeneity_seat_coefplot.pdf}
\end{center}
\smallskip
\takeaway{The winner's top-two margin widens in both seat types ($\OpenMarginCoef$, $p=\OpenMarginP$ open; $\ContMarginCoef$, $p=\ContMarginP$ contested), but in open seats the margin was already widening before 2020 ($\OpenPtMarginCoef$, pre-trend $p=\OpenPtMarginP$), while in contested seats it was not ($p=\ContPtMarginP$). In open seats it is \pos{decisiveness}: the winner crosses 50\% ($\OpenWinMajCoef$, $p=\OpenWinMajP$) with no ballot demobilization. In contested seats it carries a \negt{barrier} signature: blank votes rise ($\ContBlankCoef$, $p=\ContBlankP$) and valid votes fall ($\ContValidCoef$, $p=\ContValidP$) as voters confront an entrenched incumbent, with no majority shift ($p=\ContWinMajP$). The same shock produces decisiveness where the seat is open and withdrawal where it is defended.}\hfill\hyperlink{app:seathet}{\beamergotobutton{full table}}
\end{frame}
